Romania · Ceausescu-Era Modernism

BUCHAREST

Socialist Architecture · 1950–1989

Bucharest is the clearest case in Europe of a city rewritten by political will. Under Nicolae Ceaușescu's programme of sistematizare — systematisation — roughly a fifth of the historic centre was demolished during the 1980s. Churches, hospitals and entire neighbourhoods were destroyed, or in a few cases physically moved on rails, to clear ground for the Civic Centre and the boulevard leading to it.

At the end of that boulevard stands the Palace of the Parliament: the heaviest building on earth, the largest administrative building after the Pentagon, and still unfinished when the regime fell in 1989. But Bucharest's socialist architecture is not only that. The modernism of the 1960s — Tehnoimport, the Politehnica metro station, the state circus — belongs to a briefer and more open period, before systematisation began.
