Travel journal · March 2026

BelgradeSerbia  ·  Yugoslav modernism in the rain

Photographs and text by Dicky Chan · shot on iPhone, on foot

I came in on the overnight bus from Budapest, in the state overnight buses produce: half slept, woken somewhere around two for a passport stamp, put back on board, and finally set down in the city at first light with my body arriving noticeably before the rest of me.

The right weather, by accident

The sky was low and spitting rain, and somewhere across all that wet grey stood the three chimneys of a cylindrical factory. It woke me up completely. That was the thing I had come for, and it was there in the first ten minutes.

It then rained for essentially all three days. As a backpacker that is miserable — shoes soaked, half dried, soaked again, until you begin to offend yourself. For this subject it is close to ideal. Shadowless light does not flatter concrete, and concrete does not need flattering: wet, it darkens, and the grain of the shuttering comes up honestly.

Across the Sava

New Belgrade is on the other side of the river and, in effect, the other side of the century. The old city has cobbles and churches. Over the bridge there are boulevards too wide to cross comfortably, squares built at parade scale, and one concrete mass after another under a grey ceiling.

The Eastern City Gates are three residential towers standing shoulder to shoulder, their floor plans cut and rotated into a cross, twenty-odd storeys of concrete that would look austere in any weather and looked severe in that one. I stood underneath with rain coming in sideways and felt appropriately small.

Free trams, and faces that do not smile

Some of Belgrade's trams are genuinely old Yugoslav stock — rusted window frames, wood and metal worn smooth by decades of hands — and they rattle along slowly enough to feel like a different decade. They are also free, as are the buses. No barrier, no ticket, nobody checking. For a backpacker in the rain that is an unreasonably good piece of news.

The other thing three days teaches you is that people here do not smile much. It reads as cold at first, and I do not think it is. The man who insisted on a toast, the café staff who walked me to the right street — the warmth was all there, it simply was not on the surface. A smile that is not automatic is worth more when you get one.

我是坐從布達佩斯開來的過夜巴士到的，狀態就是過夜巴士會造就的那種：半睡半醒，凌晨兩點左右被叫醒蓋章，再被趕回車上，最後在天剛亮時被放到城裡——身體明顯比意識早一步抵達。

誤打誤撞遇上對的天氣

天很低，飄著細雨，而在那一整片濕漉漉的灰之中，遠遠立著一座圓柱工廠的三根煙囪。我整個人瞬間醒了。我來這裡要找的就是那個東西，而它在最初十分鐘就出現了。

接下來三天，雨幾乎沒停過。對背包客來說這相當狼狽——鞋子濕了半乾、再濕，到後來連自己都嫌棄。但對這個題材而言，這幾乎是理想條件。沒有影子的光線不會美化混凝土，而混凝土也不需要被美化：淋濕之後它會變深，木模板的紋理會誠實地浮出來。

過薩瓦河

新貝爾格勒在河的另一邊，實際上也在世紀的另一邊。舊城有鵝卵石和教堂；過了橋，是寬到不好過馬路的大道、以閱兵尺度建造的廣場，以及灰色天花板下一棟接一棟的混凝土巨體。

東城門是三座並肩而立的住宅塔樓，平面被切割、旋轉成十字形，二十來層的混凝土在任何天氣下都顯得冷峻，在那天則是嚴厲。我站在底下，雨橫著打過來，人小得剛剛好。

免費的電車，和不笑的臉

貝爾格勒有些電車是真正的南斯拉夫年代舊車——生鏽的窗框、被幾十年的手掌磨得光滑的木頭與金屬——行駛起來哐噹作響，慢得像還活在另一個年代。而且它們是免費的，巴士也是。沒有閘機、沒有票、沒有人查。對一個在雨裡的背包客來說，這是好得不太合理的消息。

三天還教會你另一件事：這裡的人不太笑。一開始你會覺得冷，但我不認為那是冷。那個硬要跟我乾杯的大叔、那位帶我走到對的街口的咖啡館店員——溫度一直都在，只是不放在表面。一個不是自動給出的笑容，拿到的時候份量更重。

Practical guide — 2026

Conditions here change with politics. Check current advice before you travel; this reflects a June 2026 visit and sources current at that time.

Public transport is free — with exceptions

Since 1 January 2025 Belgrade's buses, trams, trolleybuses and the BG Train have been free for everyone, with no ticket and regardless of nationality. The exceptions matter on arrival: the A1 airport express and the E minibus lines are not part of the free system and still need a paid ticket.

Arriving

Overnight buses connect Belgrade with Budapest and much of the region, and cross the border in the small hours — keep your passport where you can reach it without unpacking.

Weather and light

Overcast is not a problem for this subject; it is the point. Flat grey light and wet concrete give better texture than sunshine. Waterproof footwear is the one thing worth getting right.

How long

Three days: one for Novi Beograd, one for the rest of the modernist landmarks, one to stop chasing buildings and sit somewhere.
